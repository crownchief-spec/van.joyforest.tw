01

Driving, cameras & keys

  1. The campervan is about 3 m high. Avoid underground car parks, low awnings, signs and branches. If clearance is uncertain, stop and check or take another route.
  2. The body extends behind the rear wheels and swings outward in a turn. Turn and reverse slowly, leaving extra room at both sides and the right rear corner.
  3. Camera views, left to right: rear-facing, rear overhead and right rear corner. Cameras are aids. Stop and check outside when unsure; use a spotter standing safely clear if needed.
  4. There are 3 front and 3 rear seats in the driving cab. Everyone must use a designated seat and seat belt while moving. Use the lounge and beds only when parked.
  5. Keys in the photo, left to right: driving cab, rear camper door and water cap. Turn the cab key toward the front to unlock and toward the rear to lock. Check each door before leaving.
  6. Secure luggage in unused cab space. For music: Home → Apps → Bluetooth Phone → pair your phone.
Published dimensions are approximately 5 m long and 2 m wide. Check the full vehicle outline when parking and keep traffic and pavements clear. See the four safety cards for downhill and other driving advice.

02

Awning, side table & entry

  1. Confirm that the venue allows awnings, tables and outdoor equipment, then park securely. Follow signs at ordinary parking spaces and keep equipment within permitted areas.
  2. The crank and two support poles are under the rear cab seat. Hook the crank into the awning eye and turn slowly to extend.
  3. Support the awning with the supplied poles and check stability. Retract it in strong wind or unsettled weather, before sleeping or when leaving it unattended.
  4. Use the fold-down side table only when parked and permitted. Before driving, stow the table, awning, supports and crank, and check every latch.
  5. Fully open the folding step on firm, level ground. Remove shoes on the entrance mat. Stow and secure the step before driving.
The awning is not a storm shelter. Extending it only a little does not replace venue permission. Follow site rules for all outdoor equipment.

03

Fresh water, diesel & AdBlue

  1. The fresh-water inlet is the round cap marked WATER on the right side. Unlock it with the water-cap key and use the clean supplied hose with a suitable fresh-water source.
  2. The tank holds about 110 L. Check the indoor gauge first. Stay with the van during filling, watch the level, then stop the water, stow the hose and lock the cap.
  3. At a campsite, ask which tap to use. Elsewhere, ask the fuel-station staff before refuelling whether suitable water is available. Try another refill point if needed.
  4. Do not use unknown wash water or recycled water. Keep the hose clean. Do not treat the onboard tank water as drinking water.
  5. The vehicle takes DIESEL. Its fuel filler is on the right, by the passenger side. Give the cab key to the attendant and request a full tank of diesel.
  6. The blue cap is for AdBlue/DEF, not water or petrol. Diesel, AdBlue and fresh water have different inlets. If unsure, stop and send us a photo.
Putting water into the AdBlue tank can damage the vehicle. Let the fuel attendant handle diesel; refill fresh water only at the confirmed WATER inlet.

04

Shore power & generator

  1. The charging lead, hose and generator fuel container are in the left-rear storage box. The white two-pin plug charges the campervan.
  2. For shore power, confirm the campsite voltage, outlet and current limit. Fully uncoil the extension lead, keep connections dry, then connect the white plug to the site supply.
  3. The same plug connects to the generator AC outlet. Disconnect loads first. Follow 1 → 2 → 3 → 4: pull CHOKE for a cold start, switch ON, pull the starter, then connect only after stable running.
  4. After a cold start, return the choke as shown at handover. To stop, unplug the charging lead first, then switch to STOP.
  5. The generator uses 92-octane unleaded petrol, not the vehicle’s diesel. Stop and cool it before refuelling. Use the supplied container; add slowly without exceeding the tank limit. Never fill it directly with a fuel-station nozzle.
  6. Check venue permission, noise and exhaust conditions. Keep exhaust away from doors, windows and nearby vehicles. Stop, leave the area and contact us if you smell fumes, see a fuel leak or hear a CO alarm.
Use only while parked and after confirming the manufacturer’s ventilation and exhaust clearances. Never run it in living spaces, tents or an enclosed compartment. An open door alone does not establish safe ventilation. If unsure, do not start it; send us a photo. Stop it before driving.

05

Power panel, pump & charging rate

  1. At the right of the camper entrance: the round gauge shows fresh water, the centre screen is the battery monitor, and the row below contains equipment switches.
  2. The first four switches from the left control lights; the second is the exterior light and the fifth is the fridge. Switch WATER PUMP on before using a tap, then off after use or before leaving.
  3. While driving, turn off unused lights and the pump. The fridge may stay on. Check fridge and cupboard latches before departure.
  4. At handover, the calibrated % and Ah give a useful starting reading; full capacity is set to about 800 Ah. After repeated charging cycles, compare voltage and its trend with the chart beside the panel.
  5. A is net current: positive means charging, negative means consumption. W is instant power. A large load such as A/C can produce a negative reading even while connected to power.
  6. To reduce charging load, open the smallest cupboard below the panel and find the blue knob at the lower right. Reduce appliances first, lower charging current, then watch the A reading.
  7. Recharge early when using A/C and avoid dropping below 10%. Do not run high-power appliances together. The fitted chart gives approximate references: 13.5 V ≥90%, 13.4 V 80%, 13.3 V 70%, 13.2 V 60%.
Voltage changes with charging and load. The chart is an operating reference, not an exact state-of-charge measurement. Send us the panel and appliance status if readings look abnormal.

06

Storage, fridge & entertainment

See the projector, USB-C to HDMI and speaker AUX photo guide →

  1. The tall cupboard by the entrance holds the camping table and chairs. Keep the aisle clear and return clean equipment to its original place.
  2. Upper cupboards hold the Switch, cables and projector fittings, followed by picnic fabric, mats, string lights, bedding, sheets and pillowcases.
  3. The cupboards above the fridge hold the picnic basket, Bluetooth speaker, projector, stand and hair dryer. Follow your handover inventory for included items.
  4. The upper fridge compartment is the freezer; the lower one is the fridge. Turn its panel switch on, secure food and latch the door. There is currently no microwave.
  5. Secure the projector on its stand, then connect an HDMI source such as the Switch. USB-C phones must support video output; streaming services may restrict playback. Select HDMI audio or the Bluetooth speaker as appropriate.
  6. Keep electronics and string lights dry. Close and latch each cupboard after use and check them all before driving.
Bring a personal pillow if you prefer a larger one. Ask before using high-power cooking appliances such as rice cookers, induction hobs or air fryers.

07

Toilet, treatment & waste cassette

  1. Once parked, switch on the bathroom light and rotate the toilet to a usable position. Open the blade valve as demonstrated and check the central opening before use.
  2. Flush after use, close the blade valve and toilet lid. Never put wet wipes, sanitary products, plastic or other foreign items into the toilet.
  3. Add treatment after emptying the cassette, not after every toilet visit. The video demonstrates about 100–200 mL; follow the supplied product concentration, label and handover dose.
  4. Close the blade valve before removal. Open the low white service hatch by the entrance, lift the retaining catch and pull the cassette out level. Do not force a stuck cassette.
  5. Empty only at an approved chemical-toilet or waste-disposal point. Turn out the spout, remove its cap, empty and rinse as directed, then close, reinstall and check the retaining catch.
Never empty waste onto roads, soil, storm drains or ordinary sinks. Public toilets may not accept chemical-toilet waste; get the operator’s permission and follow chemical restrictions.

08

Shower, toiletries & blinds

  1. Check fresh water, switch the pump on and open the tap. The bathroom tap pulls out as a shower head. Adjust and test the temperature before showering.
  2. Use a short, stop-start shower: wet, turn water off to wash, then rinse. Use campsite showers when available to conserve the onboard supply.
  3. If hot water fails, temperature is abnormal or the pump runs continuously, close the tap and switch the pump off. Contact us; do not alter gas fittings or wiring.
  4. Towels and disposable toothbrushes are in the cupboard above the bathroom. Close it after use. Dry pooled water, ventilate and switch the pump off before leaving.
  5. Press the grey catch and move the blinds gently: blackout above, insect screen below. Support the rails with both hands. Shut and latch windows before driving.
The fresh-water tank is about 110 L, so avoid long continuous showers. Follow the handover demonstration for hot water and gas appliances.

09

Dinette bed & fixed upper bed

  1. Park securely and apply the parking brake. Clear the table and put cushions aside. Keep objects away from the table mechanism.
  2. The tabletop moves forward, back and sideways. Centre it so it can lower without catching the seats.
  3. Hold the release control on the table pedestal and guide the top down slowly with both hands, until level with the side supports. Keep fingers clear of moving and pinching points.
  4. Check that the tabletop and supports are stable. Move the seat cushions inward, fill the centre with the narrow cushion and place backrest cushions at the sides to make a flat bed.
  5. The upper bed stays in place. Reverse the process to restore the dinette, supporting the top and raising it as demonstrated. Do not force the mechanism or stand on the table.
Beds are for parked use only. Nobody may travel in the bed or lounge. Restore and secure the table and cushions before driving.

10

Power reset & departure checklist

  1. If power cuts out, turn off high-load appliances such as the dryer and A/C first. Check battery level and shore power. Do not keep forcing restarts.
  2. For an inverter protection trip, follow handover instructions: lift the right-side seat-base cover, locate the silver inverter’s external 0/1 switch, switch to 0, then back to 1. Do not touch terminals or wiring.
  3. Reconnect essential appliances one at a time. If it trips again, smells burnt, overheats or makes an unusual sound, stop using it and contact us. Refit covers and keep cooling space clear.
  4. Before every drive, stow the awning, supports, side table, step, hose and extension. Unplug shore power and latch service doors, windows, fridge and cupboards.
  5. Seat and belt everyone in the driving cab, secure luggage and check around and underneath the vehicle before moving slowly away.
  6. Before return, remove personal items and rubbish, clean and stow equipment, refill diesel as agreed and confirm the return place and time. Report faults or damage early.
For help, park safely and send photos of the equipment location, panel readings and fault via LINE or WhatsApp. Leave the area and seek emergency help for immediate danger.
