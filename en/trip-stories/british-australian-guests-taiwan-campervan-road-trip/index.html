A British Traveler from Australia Discovers Taiwan by Campervan

A British traveler based in Australia stopped merely transiting through Taiwan and explored it for the first time—from Taipei's airport gateway to the coast, mountains, Sun Moon Lake and a final half-day in Taipei.

2026-08-20 · 10 min read ·British traveler, Australia, Taiwan road trip, Taipei travel, international guests, Taiwan campervan rental
中文

Beginning | His first real visit to Taiwan began with the sea, mountains and a lake

Some travelers pass through Taiwan many times without ever leaving the airport. Daniel (a pseudonym) is a British traveler who works in Australia and moves between Australia and the United Kingdom. He had often transited through Taiwan; this time, he and his travel companion Sophie (also a pseudonym) decided to stop merely passing through and experience the island for the first time.

Their choice was wonderfully different: collect a campervan at Taoyuan International Airport, follow the coast east, sleep beside the mountains and cross the highlands to Sun Moon Lake. A first visit to Taiwan does not have to mean staying only in Taipei. With their room, table and luggage traveling with them, they crossed half of Taiwan and met the island through its coastline, mountain roads, lakes and generous people.

Development | Coastal roads, mountain camps and someone to ask along the way

Before arrival, we spent a great deal of time discussing the campervan, transport, route, equipment and camping rhythm in English. For international guests driving in Taiwan for the first time, renting the vehicle is only one part of the journey. It also matters to know that someone can help when local information is difficult to read, a campsite cannot be reached, or an arrival time suddenly changes.

For this trip, I contacted and booked three campsites and shared ideas for coastal stops, food and routes. Campsite owners used translation software to chat with the guests. Language did not keep people apart; it became part of a warm and memorable exchange.

Our campervan plan includes unlimited mileage, so guests do not need to abandon a meaningful destination simply because it is farther away. You can plan a Taiwan round-island journey or spend several days exploring only the east coast, mountains or lakes. Weather, road access, legal overnight locations and driving experience must always guide the final plan—but the radius of the adventure can remain wonderfully open.

Sea | Following the northeast coast beside the Pacific

They drove over elevated freeways and bridges and through tunnel after tunnel. At Qingshui Cliff, enormous rock walls fell toward a turquoise sea; the road disappeared into the mountain, then emerged to reveal the Pacific again. In his review, Daniel remembered the roads, temples, inexpensive seafood and consistently friendly people as part of an extraordinary east-coast experience.

Mountains | Seven hours across the highlands, with Sun Moon Lake beyond

The most demanding—and most memorable—part of Daniel's journey was a seven-hour mountain drive. Before departure, everyone was watching a typhoon warning and changing road conditions. The route also required attention to timed access, weather, falling rocks, altitude and the campervan's height. Then the landscape began to unfold: valleys, bridges, gorges and high mountain horizons.

That day he messaged, “We made it over the pass—an incredible journey.” At Sun Moon Lake, they ate outdoors with the water before them. The weather that had worried us became remarkably kind, and their photographs of sea, mountains and sunset allowed me to see Taiwan again through their eyes.

Lake | Letting a Sun Moon Lake sunset close the mountain crossing

At Sun Moon Lake, they hired bicycles, took the cable car and watched the sun fall across the water. Daniel called the seven-hour high pass the highlight of the trip. When the campervan was parked, the table and chairs were outside and dinner was served beside the lake, the sunset felt like a quiet seal on the long journey.

Turn | After returning the campervan, half a day still belonged to Taipei

We completed the vehicle return near the airport, but their flight was not leaving immediately. I did not want luggage and backtracking to consume their final hours, so I suggested checking out first, taking the Taoyuan Airport MRT to Taipei Main Station, leaving the bags in a locker and changing to the Taipei MRT Red Line for Taipei 101/World Trade Center Station.

The half-day idea included the Taipei 101 Observatory and xiaolongbao at Din Tai Fung, followed—if time allowed—by YouBike rides to Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall and Songshan Cultural and Creative Park. In reality, Din Tai Fung had a 45-minute wait and they were too hungry to queue, but they did visit the observatory and see Taipei from above. They then returned to Taipei Main Station, collected their bags and took the Airport MRT to the terminal.

That is part of the help we enjoy providing: not only campervan explanations and campsite contact, but also thinking through how the complete Taiwan itinerary can flow. Even after the vehicle is returned, a few well-planned hours can become one more Taipei memory.

Resolution | Their photographs helped me see Taiwan again

Back in Australia, Daniel sent a long email. He described the week as “a huge success and a wonderful experience,” said the three campsites were excellent choices and wrote, “It was all so perfect.” His closing promise was simple: “I'll be back!” It was more than a polite farewell—he genuinely wants to return and leave southern Taiwan for the next journey.

This time they followed the northeast coast, crossed the mountains and reached Sun Moon Lake: a road trip across half of Taiwan. Tainan and Kenting remain on the next page of the map. A future route can linger among Tainan's old streets, temples, food and deep cultural history, then continue south to Kenting's tropical sunshine, blue water and beaches. Perhaps not seeing everything in one visit is exactly what gives a traveler a reason to come back.

Looking through their photographs of the coast, gorges, Sun Moon Lake and the campervan, I felt as though they had taken me around Taiwan again. We had worried about the typhoon at first, but the weather stayed remarkably kind. The light and happiness in the pictures made their joy easy to feel. Being able to offer help across unfamiliar language and roads—and later receiving the words “I'll be back”—is one of the warmest parts of this work.
