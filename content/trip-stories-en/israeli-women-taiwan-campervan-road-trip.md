## “Taiwan—the trip of a lifetime” | Two women, nine days, one moving home

When **Maya and Noa (pseudonyms)** arrived from Israel, they already knew what they wanted from Taiwan: beautiful beaches, dramatic scenery, good food and a trip that felt lived—not a checklist of photo stops.

They are also travel and lifestyle content creators. Before departure, they wanted to film the details of real van life: waking up inside the campervan, cooking beside it, scenic drives, interior routines and practical tips. One Hebrew video caption roughly called Taiwan “the destination of a lifetime, with much more from this trip still to come.” Another said that every morning of the journey felt more perfect than the last.

Their route began in Taipei, followed Taiwan’s east coast through Dong’ao and Hualien, crossed by ferry to Green Island, continued through Zhiben and Kenting, then turned north for two nights beside Sun Moon Lake. The rhythm mattered: scenic coastal stops were alternated with proper campsites for water, electricity and showers, while hot springs and local food gave the drive room to breathe.

The southern Hualien hot-spring corridor and Kenting section include places that were genuinely compared and called, but the final check-in could not be verified; those are labelled as options rather than presented as confirmed stays.

## Day 1 | From the Taipei handover straight to a seaside first night in Dong’ao

After the September 7 handover and equipment walkthrough in Taipei, they drove down the northeast coast. They moved faster than the original Yilan plan and reached Dong’ao, near the Yilan–Hualien boundary, for their first night at **[Sleep by Sea](https://sleepyseaside.com/)**. We prepaid the NT$1,000 campsite fee and coordinated their late arrival with the owner.

The campsite is close to Dong’ao Bay and **[Fenniaolin Fishing Harbor and beach](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Fenniaolin+Fishing+Harbor)**. ATV forest routes, SUP and river-floating activities can also be discussed locally. A formal campsite was ideal for the first night: it gave them time to learn the external power, water, shower and storage systems before trying more independent stops.

## Day 2 | Fenniaolin, Qingshui Cliff and Ocean Chill in Hualien

There was no reason to return north to Jiaoxi. From Dong’ao they continued south, stopping for the morning at Fenniaolin before following the Suhua coast to the **[Qingshui Cliff viewpoint](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Qingshui+Cliff+viewpoint)**. The useful lesson here is not to overload the day: if the beach morning runs late, choose either Qingshui Cliff or a half-day Taroko visit and enjoy it properly.

That evening we arranged **[Ocean Chill](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ocean+Chill+campground+Hualien)** in Shun’an Village near Mambo Beach. The recorded rate for this trip was NT$500, with external electricity available; because it was a weekday, they were the only guests that night. Their arrival message was wonderfully simple: **“We arrive!! It’s good place.”**

Dinner was at **[Hualien Dongdamen Night Market](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Hualien+Dongdamen+Night+Market)**—street food and games after a day on one of Taiwan’s most dramatic coastal roads.

## Days 3–4 | Leave the campervan at Fugang and take the ferry to Green Island

South of Hualien, Taiwan’s Rift Valley offers hot springs around Ruisui, Hongye and Zhiben. We checked two different arrangements during this trip: **Sanchieh Happy Campground**, a simple and economical site with a hot spring across the road, and **East Rift Valley / Toyugi Hot Spring Resort**, which had better resort facilities but no electrical hook-up at its campervan spaces. These were genuine options discussed during the journey; the final overnight message was not preserved, so neither is presented here as a confirmed stay.

On September 10, they left the campervan at a long-stay parking area near **[Fugang Fishing Harbor](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Fugang+Fishing+Harbor+Taitung)** and took the ferry to Green Island. This avoids the cost and complication of transporting a vehicle. Sailings can be cancelled in strong wind, so travelers should arrive early—or buy the next morning’s ticket the day before—and always check the operator’s same-day notice.

The island’s signature experience is **[Zhaori Hot Spring](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Zhaori+Hot+Spring+Green+Island)**, where geothermal water emerges beside the sea. You can wait for sunrise while soaking beside the Pacific. Public pools require swimwear and usually a swim cap—a small Taiwan-specific detail international visitors may not expect.

## Day 5 | Do not force the drive to Kenting—eat in Taitung, then soak in Zhiben

The ferry returned to Fugang in the afternoon. Driving straight to Kenting would have meant arriving very late, so this became a Taitung recovery day: walk around **[Tiehua Music Village and Railway Art Village](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tiehua+Music+Village+Taitung)**, browse local design shops and eat dinner featuring ingredients and flavors from Taiwan’s Indigenous east coast.

After dinner, they drove about half an hour in the correct southbound direction to **[Dawang Mountain Residence Campground](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Dawang+Mountain+Residence+Campground+Zhiben)** in Zhiben. We confirmed that the owner would receive them after 18:00. The weekend campervan rate recorded for this trip was NT$1,200, including access to electricity and water; the bathrooms were confirmed clean. They were the only guests that night, and the owner prepared a private hot-spring room.

This was an important reset: the previous days involved coast roads and a ferry, with Kenting still ahead. A quiet forest campsite with a proper shower, power, water and hot spring was more valuable than another night drive.

## Day 6 | Hengchun and Kenting: water first, then choose facilities or coastal atmosphere

Zhiben to Kenting is a long drive, so the practical choice was to follow navigation south rather than add detours. Before the beaches, the road reaches **[Hengchun Old Town](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Hengchun+Old+Town)**, a lively place for food, supplies and historic gates. Travelers interested in marine life can make the **[National Museum of Marine Biology and Aquarium](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=National+Museum+of+Marine+Biology+Taiwan)** their first stop. Snorkeling or diving can be reserved on Klook or with local operators for that day or the next morning.

Three campsites were actually called and compared. The surviving messages conflict about the final check-in, so the honest record is a practical comparison:

- **[O’Glamping Maobitou](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=O%27Glamping+Maobitou+Kenting)** had the most complete facilities. The September 2026 weekend quote was NT$1,500, but arrival had to be before 17:00.
- **[Kenting Qingliang Resort Campground](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kenting+Qingliang+Resort+Campground)** was simpler and more flexible near Shadao and Eluanbi. The phone estimate was NT$500–700 with water, power and showers.
- **[Shadao Campground](https://maps.app.goo.gl/bNqCjcrueSM4dhvu6?g_st=iw)** quoted NT$600 for two people plus NT$150 for electricity. Water and power were available and the location was close to Kenting, but the bathrooms were basic.

These are **historical quotes from September 2026**, not permanent prices. Future guests should reconfirm availability, access for a vehicle about 5m long and 2.8–3m high, electricity, water, check-in deadline and current fees.

## Days 7–8 | From Taiwan’s far south to two lake-view nights at Sun Moon Lake

Kenting to Sun Moon Lake takes roughly five to six hours with stops. Because two nights were booked at the lake, there was no need to leave at dawn. A morning water activity in Kenting, lunch around Chaozhou, then a steady drive north on the western freeway made a more balanced day.

We prepaid two nights in the Lake View B area at **[Tiepoka Campground](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tiepoka+Campground+Sun+Moon+Lake)**: NT$1,000 on September 13 and again on September 14, NT$2,000 total. The campsite sits beside Sun Moon Lake and within walking distance of the cable car, making it a good base for cycling, lake views and a genuine slow day without moving the vehicle.

Tiepoka has no conventional hotel reception, so guests call the owner on arrival. Its electrical rules prohibit high-draw appliances above 500W. Campervan travelers should not assume the air conditioner and charger can always run together: first confirm the campsite circuit capacity, then reduce the load if charging pauses.

## Real van life | Morning routines, outdoor meals and practical problem-solving

Their content did not show only landmarks. It included getting ready beside a campervan window, making the double bed, cooking breakfast outdoors and managing water and electricity. Those moments answer the question potential guests really have: what does it feel like to live in the vehicle for nine days?

One evening, external power was present and all internal appliances worked, but the charging display showed no incoming charge while the air conditioner was running. Once the air conditioner was switched off, charging resumed. The experience improved our own guidance: on a low-capacity campsite circuit, air-conditioner startup and the charger can create voltage drop or protection behavior. Reducing the load before checking breakers is the right first step.

## If you want to follow this route | Four accommodation anchors worth copying

1. **Sleep by Sea, Dong’ao**—learn the vehicle on night one, then continue to Fenniaolin and the Suhua coast.
2. **Ocean Chill, Hualien**—rest and recharge after Qingshui Cliff, then visit Dongdamen Night Market.
3. **Dawang Mountain Residence, Zhiben**—after Green Island, do not force the Kenting drive; recover with hot spring, clean facilities, water and electricity.
4. **Two nights at Tiepoka, Sun Moon Lake**—after the long northbound drive, keep a full day for cycling, the cable car and the lake.

Together these anchors divide the journey into manageable sections: northeast coast, Hualien, Taitung and the tropical south, then Taiwan’s central lake region. Attractions can change with weather without breaking the accommodation structure.

## What remained was more than a collection of check-in photos

Before departure, Maya and Noa said they loved “beautiful beaches, stunning scenic views, and great food.” Nine days later, their media contained all of those—but also breakfast fresh from the pan, songs during the drive, a quiet mountain campsite, hot-spring evenings and a different landscape outside the window each morning.

Supporting international guests is not simply handing over a key. English guidance, international driving permits, campsite calls, ferry schedules, swim caps, electrical hook-ups and route changes can each determine whether visitors spend the trip worrying or truly enjoying Taiwan.

Perhaps the best summary is the effortless laughter in their video: Taiwan was far from home, but once its ocean, mountains, hot springs and campervan life were joined into one route, it became a story they could not wait to keep sharing.
